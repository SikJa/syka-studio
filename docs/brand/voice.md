# Language and voice guide

Use this guide for what the work says and how it says it. Apply it to titles, labels, scripts,
captions, calls to action, and descriptions.

## Names

Take names, taglines, and descriptions from the request or the supplied material, and keep their
spelling and capitalization exact.

## Writing

- Lead with the result or action.
- Use short words, short sentences, and active voice.
- Cut any word that adds no meaning.
- State what the subject does, without hype.
- Keep code, names, technical terms, and interface labels exact.
- Avoid familiar figures of speech and vague claims.
- Rewrite copy that breaks a length limit. Do not shrink the type.
- Break a writing rule when accuracy or clarity requires it.

Review every prose output against these rules before delivering it.

## Tone

Default to clear, calm, and sure, and let the work supply the energy. Match the register of the
supplied footage or script when it has one: a playful piece may be playful. Do not sound grand,
vague, or eager to impress.

Avoid claims such as `effortless`, `magical`, `revolutionary`, or `all-in-one` unless the
supplied material makes and supports them.

## Calls to action

Write a direct action that matches the destination. Name the action rather than promising an
outcome. Keep it short enough to work without punctuation.

## Spoken copy

Apply the same writing and tone rules to scripts and narration. Take delivery and pronunciation
from the supplied material; when there is none, keep the read plain.
